## 2. Reading & Study Priorities

The readings have been triaged into three categories: **Focus**, **Skim**, and **Skip**.

### Tier 1: Focus (4-6 hours)

This is the most critical area for deep study. You have a general understanding of the identity theory, but Kripke's specific objections are nuanced and frequently appear on exams.

*   **Readings:**
    *   `readings/12_kripke_naming_and_necessity.pdf`
    *   `papers/Kripke - Naming and Necessity.pdf`

*   **Why it's important:** Kripke's argument against identity theory uses the concepts of "rigid designators" and "contingent identity" vs. "necessary identity." It's a powerful and subtle argument that goes beyond the more intuitive "multiple realizability" objection. Understanding this is key to a sophisticated grasp of the debate.

*   **Key Questions to Consider:**
    *   What does Kripke mean by a "rigid designator"? Why are names like "water" and "H₂O" rigid designators?
    *   Why does Kripke believe that identity statements between two rigid designators must be *necessary* if they are true at all?
    *   How does he apply this to the statement "Pain is C-fiber stimulation"? Why does he argue this identity cannot be necessary, and therefore cannot be true?
    *   How is this different from a scientific identity like "Heat is mean kinetic energy"?

*   **Connections:** This argument is a direct and powerful critique of the Mind-Brain Identity Theory (`readings/10_smart...`, `readings/11_place...`) which you can now safely skip.
